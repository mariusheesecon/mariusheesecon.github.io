About

I grew up in a small town in southern Germany, and my life since has moved through many places: Hamburg, Munich, Vancouver, Oxford, Boston, Chicago, Bangkok, Copenhagen, and now Rotterdam. What has stayed constant is a fascination with countries, regions, and the people who live in them, and an insatiable curiosity for languages, cultures, and histories. The places I have lived and worked are where my research questions come from: what makes, breaks, and shapes a place is, for me, both a personal and a professional question.

Stops along the way

Boston. As an undergraduate I spent an exchange year at Harvard, where I studied with Dale Jorgenson and Michael Sandel and took coursework at MIT. Cambridge also gave me one of my favorite jobs to date: teaching sailing on the Charles River as an instructor at Harvard.

Chicago. In 2024 I was a visiting PhD student at the University of Chicago, hosted by Esteban Rossi-Hansberg, where I also learned from James Robinson. The visit shaped the quantitative spatial backbone of my dissertation.

Bangkok. At the United Nations ESCAP, in the Transport Connectivity and Logistics Section, I worked on cross-national rail connectivity across the Asian continent, an early encounter with the infrastructure networks that now run through my research agenda. Living in Thailand also set a high bar for what a bowl of boat noodles should taste like.

Tehran. A research stay at the University of Tehran, collecting data on local labor market dynamics, took me to a country whose history, hospitality, and cuisine left a lasting impression.

Copenhagen. When my partner did her PhD in Copenhagen, I worked from the University of Copenhagen and Copenhagen Business School, and the city became one of our homes.

Off the desk

Sailing. I love challenging, mainly small-boat sailing, the kind where you wear the weather rather than watch it, and I taught it as a sailing instructor at Harvard.

Food. I cook as enthusiastically as I eat. The map of my kitchen follows the map of my life: green curry and boat noodles from Thailand; fesenjan from my time in Tehran; the Italian cooking of my Oxford housemates; the hearty cuisine of my home region in southern Germany, born of a once poor and infertile corner of Baden-Württemberg; and, honoring my French mother and my Copenhagen years, French cooking and the New Nordic kitchen.

Art and history. I am drawn above all to ancient history. I have traveled to see all the ancient wonders of the world, or what remains of them, and the great towns of antiquity across North Africa, the Mediterranean, the Middle East, China, and South America. Standing in what remains of a once-great city is, perhaps unsurprisingly, a spatial economist's kind of tourism.

Football, tennis, and squash keep me moving, football having been the constant since childhood.

All of it feeds back into the work: the societies, infrastructure, trade routes, cities, and migrations I have encountered firsthand are the raw material of the questions I study, and a steady source of inspiration for current and future research projects.
